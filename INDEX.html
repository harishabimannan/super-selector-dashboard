Model transparency

Methodology & evidence

The pressure label is analyst-defined. The score balances performance under pressure, change against a player-specific baseline and consistency.

Auditable framework

Pressure classification

Delivery-level match-state rules
01Chase urgency

Second innings: required run rate ≥ 9.5, or at least 2.0 above current run rate.

02Wicket pressure

Second innings: five or fewer wickets remaining after 12 legal overs.

03First-innings finish

From over 16 with five or fewer wickets remaining, or over 18 regardless of wickets.

04Player baseline

Pressure performance is compared with the same player’s non-pressure deliveries in the analysis sample.

Scoring weights

Percentile-normalised metrics within eligible role pools
Batters · 100%

Pressure strike rate 30% · boundary rate 15% · inverse dot rate 15% · inverse dismissal rate 15% · strike-rate improvement 15% · consistency 10%.

Bowlers · 100%

Inverse pressure economy 30% · wickets per 100 legal balls 20% · dot-ball rate 15% · inverse boundary rate 10% · economy improvement 15% · consistency 10%.

Eligibility gates

Batters: ≥200 pressure legal balls, ≥15 pressure matches and ≥120 baseline legal balls. Bowlers: ≥180 pressure legal balls, ≥15 pressure matches and ≥120 baseline legal balls.

Dataset coverage & caveats

Source-snapshot coverage used by this dashboard
Interpret rankings as model-based shortlists rather than universal player-quality claims. Pressure thresholds and metric weights are analyst choices; venue, opposition strength, pitch, role and match leverage are not fully adjusted. The public dashboard includes derived top-ten results only, not the raw competition data.
